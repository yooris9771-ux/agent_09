# AI Development Research ③ Regulation and Governance

> Research date: 2026-10-04 · Topic: AI development

## 1. Korea's AI Basic Act

- Took effect 2026-01-22; fully applied from 2026-07-21 together with its enforcement decree and draft notice on operator duties.
- Alongside the EU AI Act, a leading comprehensive AI regulatory regime, and Asia's first comprehensive AI law.
- The first jurisdiction to actually impose duties on **high-impact AI** that evaluates or judges people, such as in credit, healthcare, and hiring.
- Operators must document safety and reliability measures, retain them for five years, and secure the explainability of both the AI and its training data.
- The definition of "high-impact AI" is effectively identical to the EU AI Act's "high-risk system."
- Differences: weaker enforcement through sanctions than the EU, and no mandatory pre-market certification (such as CE marking)—confirmation happens only if the operator voluntarily requests it.

## 2. EU AI Act

- In June 2026, the application date for high-risk AI obligations was pushed from August 2026 to December 2027.
- A move in the opposite direction from Korea, which brought its duties into force early.

## 3. Implications

- A gap exists between the speed of technological progress and the speed of regulatory readiness (consistent with the AI Index 2026's "governance lag" finding).
- Regulatory strength and timing differ across countries, and global companies must deal with multiple regulatory regimes.
