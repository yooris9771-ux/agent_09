# AI Development Research ① Technology and Industry Trends

> Research date: 2026-10-04 · Topic: AI development

## 1. Accelerating AI Performance

- Stanford HAI's **AI Index 2026** (published 2026-04-13, 9 chapters, 423 pages) finds that AI capability is "accelerating, not plateauing."
- Scores on the coding benchmark **SWE-bench** rose from about 60% to nearly 100% within a single year.
- Models reached or exceeded human-level performance on PhD-level science questions, multimodal reasoning, and competition mathematics.
- **The Jagged Frontier**: models that perform at International Mathematical Olympiad gold-medal level read analog clocks correctly only 50.1% of the time → capabilities are uneven.

## 2. The Frontier Model Competitive Landscape

- In 2025, GPT-5 (unifying reasoning and general-purpose tracks), the Claude Opus line (stronger coding and long-horizon agent work), and Gemini 2.5 Pro (context of up to 2 million tokens) shaped the competitive landscape for 2026.
- The performance gap between leading labs has narrowed to single-digit percentage points.
- **US–China gap effectively closed**: the lead has changed hands several times since early 2025. China leads in publication volume, citations, and patent counts, while the US leads in high-impact patents and number of top-tier models.
- Technical direction: mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures and long contexts of millions of tokens.

## 3. Agentic AI Moves into Production

- The 2026 conversation has shifted from "Can AI generate content?" to "Can autonomous agents complete multi-step tasks end to end?"
- Agents perform real work—handling support tickets, retrieving data, processing refunds, updating CRMs, submitting code pull requests—with progressively less human supervision.
- In 2025 this was mostly demos, but by mid-2026 the practicality gap had largely closed in narrow domains.
- Next challenge: **interoperability**—an "agent economy" where different agents communicate via standard protocols and exchange services.
