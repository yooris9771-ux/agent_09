# AI Development Research ④ Future Outlook and Challenges

> Research date: 2026-10-04 · Topic: AI development · Scope: public sources from 2025–2026

## 1. Forecasts for the Arrival of AGI (Artificial General Intelligence)

- Expert forecasts have moved significantly closer, and many now expect AGI in the **2030s**.
  - Median of a survey of AI safety leaders: **2033**
  - AI company leaders, researchers, and forecasting platforms (such as Metaculus) keep pulling their forecasts forward, some suggesting 2026–2032.
  - Estimates from lab leaders such as Sam Altman, Dario Amodei, and Demis Hassabis converge on the **late 2020s**.
  - One METR researcher moved their forecast from "around 2041" before 2023 to "around 2028" in the first half of 2025.
- **Caveats**
  - There is no agreed definition of AGI.
  - Many of the optimistic pre-2030 forecasts come from AI company CEOs with commercial interests. They are not an independent scientific consensus.

## 2. Directions of Technological Development

| Direction | Description |
|---|---|
| Agentification | From tools to autonomous digital workers that carry out work with budgets and responsibilities |
| Efficiency | MoE architectures, small models, stronger memory capabilities |
| Multimodality | Integrated processing of text, images, speech, and video |
| Infrastructure expansion | Data center electricity demand of about 945 TWh in 2030 (about double 2025) |

## 3. Key Risk Factors

- **Existential risk**: AI safety leaders estimate a median **25%** risk of human extinction or permanent disempowerment by the end of the century (based on an expert survey; highly uncertain).
- **Rising safety incidents**: documented AI incidents rose from 233 to 362 in one year.
- **Uneven capabilities**: models solve very hard tasks but fail at simple ones, making reliability hard to ensure.
- **Energy and supply chain bottlenecks**: shortages of transformers, gas turbines, and advanced chips; grid connection delays.
- **Employment shock**: the employment impact has turned modestly negative, and workforce reductions are expected mainly at large firms.
- **Regulatory fragmentation**: Korea, the EU, and the US differ in regulatory timelines and direction.

## 4. Response Priorities

1. **Advanced safety and evaluation systems**: safety evaluation and incident reporting that keep pace with capability gains
2. **International cooperation**: global coordination to mitigate the side effects of rapid progress
3. **Workforce transition**: reskilling, job transitions, and expanded school AI policies and education
4. **Sustainable infrastructure**: links to renewable and nuclear power, investment in power grids
5. **ROI-driven adoption**: setting AI adoption priorities based on measurable value

## Implications

- The timing of AGI is uncertain, but the steady pull-forward of forecasts is itself a signal that policy and industry preparation must speed up.
- Future competitiveness depends not only on "model performance" but on overall capability spanning **safety, energy, talent, and regulatory readiness**.
