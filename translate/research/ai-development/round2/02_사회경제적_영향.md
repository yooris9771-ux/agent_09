# AI Development Research ② Socioeconomic Impact

> Research date: 2026-10-04 · Topic: AI development · Scope: public sources from 2025–2026

## 1. Productivity: Clear Gains, Unevenly Spread

- **NBER research**: labor productivity gains are positive and expected to strengthen in 2026. However, they vary widely by sector and are concentrated in **high-skill services and finance**.
- **Cognizant, "New Work, New World 2026"** (January 2026): AI can handle **$4.5 trillion** worth of US work tasks, and **93%** of today's jobs are potentially affected. The report stresses, however, that AI does not raise productivity on its own; human involvement and adaptable operations are key to capturing the value.

## 2. Employment: "Gradual Restructuring" Rather Than "Sudden Replacement"

- **S&P Global, "The AI and Labor Landscape 2026"** (June 2026)
  - Corporate AI priorities: process efficiency **64%**, employee productivity **59%**, headcount reduction **24%**. Efficiency comes before cuts.
  - However, AI's employment impact has turned **modestly negative**, reversing the positive picture of 2025.
- **NBER**: there is little evidence of near-term declines in aggregate employment. Large firms expect AI-driven workforce reductions, while smaller firms expect modest gains.
- **ZipRecruiter, "More Jobs, Higher Bar: The 2026 AI Employer Report"**: companies are using AI to reshape teams and speed up hiring while raising the bar for employees. "More jobs, but a higher threshold."

## 3. Social Acceptance and the Perception Gap

- Generative AI reached **53% population adoption within three years**, spreading faster than the PC or the internet (Stanford AI Index 2026).
- Experts and the general public differ by **about 50 percentage points** on whether AI will help people do their jobs.
- Education is falling behind: only **half** of US middle and high schools have AI policies.

## 4. Rising Safety and Incident Risks

- Documented AI incidents rose from **233 to 362** in one year (Stanford AI Index 2026).
- The core problem identified is that **safety, policy, and education are lagging** behind rapidly improving capability.

## 5. Environmental and Resource Impact

- Data center electricity demand rose 17% in 2025 and is expected to double by 2030 (IEA).
- Competition for infrastructure such as power grids, transformers, and gas turbines is intensifying, adding strain to regional power supply.
- Tech companies have sharply increased their procurement of renewable and nuclear power, emerging as major buyers in energy markets.

## Implications

- Productivity gains accrue first to high-skill and financial sectors, so **gaps between sectors and between firm sizes** may widen.
- The employment shock remains limited, but there are signs the direction is turning negative. **Reskilling and job-transition policies** need to be prepared quickly.
- Education and safety systems lag behind rapid adoption. Closing this gap is necessary to maintain public trust.
