# AI Development Research ③ Regulation and Governance

> Research date: 2026-10-04 · Topic: AI development · Scope: public sources from 2025–2026

## 1. Korea: The AI Basic Act Takes Effect

- The **Framework Act on the Development of Artificial Intelligence and Establishment of a Foundation for Trust (AI Basic Act)** is Asia's first comprehensive AI law.
- Timeline
  - **January 22, 2026**: the Act took effect
  - **July 21, 2026**: with the enforcement decree and a draft notice on operator duties, the Act came into **full force**, including high-impact AI obligations
- Key provisions
  - It imposes obligations on **high-impact AI that judges people**, such as in credit, healthcare, and hiring. Korea is the first jurisdiction to actually enforce such duties.
  - Operators must record their safety and reliability measures in a **"safety-and-trust dossier"** and **retain it for five years**.
  - The dossier must explicitly secure the **explainability** of both the AI and its training data.
  - Violations can bring fines of up to **30 million won**.

## 2. EU: High-Risk Obligations of the AI Act Postponed

- The EU AI Act is the leading example of risk-based regulation.
- In June 2026, the EU **pushed back high-risk AI (Annex III) obligations from August 2026 to December 2027**.
- As a result, Korea moved its schedule forward while the EU delayed, leaving the two regimes **on diverging timelines**.

## 3. United States: Federal Preemption vs. State Legislation

- **Executive Order 14365, "Ensuring A National Policy Framework for AI"** (December 11, 2025)
  - Aims to limit excessive state regulation of AI.
  - Directs the Attorney General to establish an **AI Litigation Task Force** to challenge state AI laws that are unconstitutional or preempted by federal law.
  - Calls for a **federal AI legislative framework** that would preempt conflicting state rules.
  - Frames state AI laws as obstacles to US competitiveness and innovation.
- **States' response**: in the six months after the order, **29 states enacted 109** new AI laws.
  - California **SB 53**: the first US law to impose safety disclosure and governance obligations on frontier AI developers.
- An executive order alone cannot invalidate state law. State laws remain in force until Congress legislates or the courts rule.
- 2026 outlook: litigation over the scope of preemption, stronger enforcement by federal agencies, and a push for federal legislation.

## 4. Comparison of Regulatory Environments

| Category | Korea | EU | US |
|---|---|---|---|
| Basic direction | Pursues promotion and trust together | Comprehensive risk-based regulation | Innovation first, federal deregulation |
| Key 2026 change | Full enforcement of high-impact AI duties (July) | High-risk obligations delayed to December 2027 | Federal preemption EO vs. spread of state laws |
| Characteristics | Explainability documentation, five-year retention | Phased application | Patchwork of state rules |

## 5. Governance Challenges

- **Safety and policy frameworks are lagging** behind capability gains (Stanford AI Index 2026).
- Differing national timelines and directions increase the **compliance burden** on multinational companies.
- Demands for transparency and disclosure around frontier AI are growing (California SB 53, Korea's safety-and-trust dossier).

## Implications

- As the first country to enforce high-impact AI rules, Korea has become a **testing ground for regulatory models**.
- With international regulation diverging, companies need **jurisdiction-specific compliance strategies**.
- The debate over balancing innovation and safety is expected to continue through 2027.
