# AI Development Research ① Technology and Industry Trends

> Research date: 2026-10-04 · Topic: AI development · Scope: public sources from 2025–2026

## 1. AI Performance: Accelerating, Not Plateauing

- Stanford HAI's **AI Index 2026** (published April 2026) directly rebuts the "AI plateau" narrative and finds that capability is still accelerating.
- Scores on the coding benchmark **SWE-bench Verified** rose from about 60% to nearly 100% in a single year.
- Models performed at or above human level on PhD-level science questions, multimodal reasoning, and competition mathematics.
- **The Jagged Frontier**: models handle very hard tasks well but perform relatively poorly on simple ones such as reading an analog clock. In other words, their capabilities are uneven.

## 2. The Frontier Model Competitive Landscape

- The performance gap between leading labs has narrowed to single-digit percentage points, and a new flagship model ships every few weeks.
- **The US–China gap has effectively closed.** The lead has changed hands several times since early 2025. China leads in publication volume, citations, and patent counts, while the US holds the edge in high-impact patents and the number of top-tier models.
- The technical focus is shifting from "bigger models" to **efficiency and memory**. Mixture-of-experts (MoE) architectures activate only the parts a task needs, and small, efficient models are spreading.

## 3. Agentic AI

- In 2026, AI is moving from a "productivity tool" to an **autonomous system**.
- Investors expect agents to become "digital workers" with defined responsibilities and budgets, handling even financial decisions such as issuing refunds or purchasing inventory.
- Goldman Sachs named **personal agents** and corporate **mega-alliances** as key themes for 2026.

## 4. Investment and Adoption

- After years of heavy spending, companies have begun demanding **real returns (ROI)**. 2026 is being called the "show me the money" year.
- Five shifts define 2026: ① agentic AI ② multimodal models ③ small and efficient models ④ hardening governance ⑤ demand for measurable value.

## 5. Energy and Infrastructure Constraints

- Data center electricity demand rose **17%** in 2025, more than five times the 3% growth in overall global electricity demand. Demand from AI-focused data centers jumped **50%**.
- The IEA projects that data center electricity use will double from about **485 TWh** in 2025 to about **945 TWh** in 2030, and that AI-related demand will triple over the same period.
- Bottlenecks: shortages of gas turbines, transformers, and advanced chips; grid connection delays; and complex permitting.
- Responses: tech companies signed about 40% of corporate renewable power purchase agreements (PPAs) in 2025, and interest in nuclear and advanced geothermal is growing.

## 6. Korea's AI Industry

- In October 2025, NVIDIA announced it would supply **260,000 GPUs in total** to the Korean government, Samsung Electronics, SK Group, Hyundai Motor Group, and Naver Cloud (worth up to about 14 trillion won).
  - Government: up to 50,000 of the latest Blackwell GPUs (to support industry AI R&D)
  - Samsung, SK, and Hyundai: 50,000 each; Naver Cloud: 60,000
- As of July 2026, the government is pursuing a plan to put about **5 trillion won** of surplus semiconductor tax revenue into **Sovereign AI** (a Korean frontier model). Its "selection and concentration" strategy would secure about 10,000 next-generation Vera Rubin GPUs and concentrate them on a single team.

## Implications

- Performance keeps rising fast, but because capabilities are uneven ("jagged"), real-world use still needs verification.
- Competition is widening from model performance to **agent deployment, cost efficiency, ROI, and power infrastructure**.
- Korea is entering the frontier race in earnest through GPU procurement and concentrated Sovereign AI investment.
